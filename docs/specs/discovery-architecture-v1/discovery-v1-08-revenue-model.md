# 08 — Portava Revenue Model

## 1. Revenue philosophy

Portava should make money when it creates measurable travel value.

## 2. Core revenue streams

### Booking / affiliate commissions
Hotels, activities, tours, eSIM, insurance, transportation, reservations where available.

### Marketplace fees
Local experiences, guides, buddy/host services.

### Premium traveler subscription
Possible features:
- advanced AI planning,
- offline trip packs,
- route optimization,
- premium alerts,
- advanced collaboration.

### Business subscriptions
Businesses pay for:
- claimed profiles,
- analytics,
- CRM/messaging tools,
- campaign tools,
- booking integrations.

### Sponsored placements
Must be clearly labeled and separated from organic ranking.

### Creator products
Premium itineraries, Trails, guides where appropriate.

## 3. Organic integrity

Businesses cannot buy organic rank.

Sponsored inventory must:
- be labeled,
- obey safety/quality rules,
- not displace all organic inventory,
- have its own auction/selection logic.

## 4. Economic attribution

Every monetizable action should be able to link back to:
- recommendation_id,
- user/session,
- content,
- place,
- Trail,
- creator(s),
- campaign if applicable.

## 5. Marketplace economics

For paid experiences:
- gross booking value,
- Portava fee,
- taxes/fees,
- creator/host share,
- refund/chargeback reserve,
- net payout.

## 6. Business analytics

Potential paid metrics:
- profile views,
- save-to-trip rate,
- itinerary additions,
- verified visit lift,
- Trail presence,
- visitor segments in aggregate.

Avoid selling sensitive individual-level behavior.

## 7. Acceptance criteria

Revenue architecture is healthy when:
- Portava revenue aligns with traveler value,
- sponsored and organic systems are distinct,
- attribution is auditable,
- creator share can be computed from the same ledger.
