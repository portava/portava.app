Engineering Architecture & Mobile Design Specification
Canonical implementation target · Mobile-first · iOS + Android

Concept visual: Passport Home, stamps, availability, trust, My World, memories, plans and social context

1. Canonical Product Definition
Passport is Portava’s portable human identity layer. It is not merely a profile page: it is the privacy-aware, context-aware representation of a traveler that other Portava systems can query for identity, travel history, current state, availability, intent, trust, credentials, reputation, plans, shared context and permitted actions.
System shorthandPassport = WHO · Availability = WHEN · Intent = WHAT · Trust = CAN / SHOULD · Map = WHERE · Compass = WHAT NEXT
Identity + Travel History + Current State + Availability + Intent + Trust + Credentials + Reputation + Social Context + Experience History + Plans + Privacy + Viewer Relationship + Action Eligibility                                      ↓                              Passport Projection

4. Viewer Context and Server-Side Projection
Do not create separate profile systems for self, public, Trips, Buddy, Discovery or Telegraph. Build one Passport projection system with context-specific views. Privacy filtering must happen before data reaches the mobile client.
type PassportViewerContext =  | 'self' | 'public' | 'follower' | 'following'  | 'trip_crew' | 'trip_host'  | 'buddy_customer' | 'buddy_provider' | 'event_group';
Canonical User Data → Passport Context Resolver → Viewer Relationship → Privacy Policy → Trust/Eligibility Policy → Projection Builder → Passport View Model → Mobile UI
5. Current Traveler State
Separate permanent identity from temporary traveler state.
Supported examples: Home, Traveling, Exploring, Open to Plans, At Event, With Crew, Unavailable.
Every temporary state requires validFrom and expiration semantics.
Broad city context may be projected; exact location is never ordinary Passport data.

8. Open to Plans and Intent
type SocialAvailability = 'open'|'maybe'|'crew_only'|'following_only'|'not_open';
Availability answers “Can I do something?” Open to Plans answers “Do I want social invitations?”
Temporary intent is distinct from long-term interests and should carry a TTL or explicit clear action.
Current intent examples: Food, Drinks, Nightlife, Explore, Events, Meet Travelers.
Compass and Discovery should weight explicit current intent more heavily than generic interests.

Do not expose private report counts, moderation evidence or sensitive safety history to other users.
Trust confidence matters: an 82 with high evidence is not equivalent to an 82 with little evidence.
Public copy for low-evidence accounts should avoid stigmatizing new users; e.g. “New Traveler · Verified.”
Trust changes must be internally replayable from evidence/events.

13. Stamp Design
Premium collectible appearance with perforated/passport-stamp edges.
Unique country/city/event motifs rather than identical generic badges.
Issue date and verification treatment visible in detail view.
Metallic accents and subtle depth may distinguish premium/earned states.
Stamp detail can link to Journey and My World while respecting historical-location privacy.
14. Journeys and Featured Journey
Journeys are chronological Passport projections of canonical Trip/travel records.
Group by year, country/city and Trip.
Show permitted dates, places, memories, stamps, Shared Moments and people context.
Allow one Featured Journey such as “30 Days in Vietnam.”
Featured Journey can include route, timeline, places, memories, stamps, people, events and recommendations.
15. Memories and Shared Memories
Memories are travel-contextual, not a generic media grid.
Views: Trips, Places, People, Timeline and Map.
Retain permitted place, city, Trip, date, people, event and stamp context.
Shared history can surface “You were here together” or “Our Da Nang Trip” when both viewers have rights to the underlying records.
16. Plans and Trip Overlap
Passport must represent future travel as well as past travel.
Upcoming Trips require per-plan visibility controls.
When viewing another Passport, compute Trip overlap such as “You’ll both be in Bangkok Sep 14–17.”
Provide an action such as “Connect for Bangkok” when policy and eligibility permit.
17. Shared Context — ME ↔ THEM
Shared Context is calculated for the viewer relationship rather than stored as a permanent dating-style match score.
Possible Shared Context• Both in Da Nang• Both free tonight• 3 mutual follows• 2 shared cities• Nightlife + Food overlap• Both going to Bangkok• 1 previous Trip together• 2 Shared Moments
Do not create a dating match scoreUse explainable descriptions such as “Strong travel overlap” and show the contributing facts.
18. See What You Could Do — Compass Handoff
Passport A + Passport B + Availability Overlap + Intent Overlap + Trust Eligibility + Current City + Trip Constraints + Live Map → Compass → Candidate Experiences
Example result: “You’re both free 8–11 PM. Here are three things that fit both of you.” This is a core bridge from identity to real-world interaction.

23. Location Boundary
Hard rulePassport may say “Traveling in Da Nang.” It should not ordinarily expose exact coordinates or “42 meters from Sky36.” Exact location belongs to purpose-bound Presence systems such as Trip Crew, Locate My Friends, Safe Return or explicit temporary sharing.
24. Blocking and Safety Propagation
A Passport block must propagate across Discovery, Telegraph, Trips, Presence, Map, Shared Moments, Bump, Buddy and Compass social recommendations. Do not implement blocking independently per surface.
25. QR Passport and Bump
Share Passport options: QR, Share Link, Copy Link, Bump.
QR projection is deliberately minimal: photo, first name, @handle, verification, permitted home country/interests, Follow/Connect.
Scanning a QR never bypasses privacy policy.
Future Passport Bump should require affirmative exchange/confirmation; passive proximity alone must not reveal profiles.
26. My World
WORLD → Country → City → Trip → Places → Memories / Stamps
My World is the personal geographic travel-history view. Main Map answers “What is happening now?” My World answers “Where has my story happened?” They can deep-link but must not merge truth models.
27. Design System
Dark-mode first with deep navy/black surfaces.
Purple as the Passport identity accent.
Gold for premium/earned travel identity and collectible stamps.
Green for verification and positive trust states.
Blue/teal for availability and social context.
Use a travel hero image, circular overlapping portrait, rounded cards and restrained glass effects.
Color is never the only status indicator; pair it with text/iconography.

30. Actions Must Be Server-Projected
{  "actions": {    "can_follow": true,    "can_message": true,    "can_make_plan": true,    "can_invite_trip": false,    "can_view_availability": true,    "can_view_trust": true  }}
Do not let the client recreate policy with rules such as “if trust > 60 show Make Plan.” The server owns authorization and viewer-specific action eligibility.
31. Loading, Caching and Expiration
Identity → Viewer Relationship → Privacy Projection → Current State → Availability → Trust Summary → Shared Context → Stamps/History → Plans/Memories
Cache relatively static identity, stamp metadata, travel stats, travel identity and permitted public journeys.
Use short TTLs for Availability, current state, Open to Plans, Shared Context, Trust projection and capabilities.
Explicitly expire Availability, temporary intent, Open to Plans, event Passport, temporary sharing and location projections.
Never render stale Availability as current.

34. Explicit Non-Goals
Not a generic Instagram profile.
Not a dating profile or compatibility score.
Not a raw exact-location screen.
Not a place where users self-award verification.
Not a public moderation record.
Not a Trust leaderboard.
Not permanent exposure of future travel plans.
Not a duplicate database for Trips, Buddy, Memory, Map or Presence.
35. Engineering North Star
Two travelers view one another. Portava determines that both are in the same city, both explicitly free tonight, both open to plans, both interested in nightlife, eligible under trust policy, and share relevant social/travel context. Passport presents that context and offers “See What You Could Do.” Compass combines the two permitted Passport projections with live Map intelligence, proposes suitable experiences, the users form a plan, Telegraph coordinates it, and the resulting real-world experience can later become Memory, Trip history, qualified contribution or Stamp evidence.
Complete Passport loopPASSPORT → AVAILABILITY → TRUST → SHARED CONTEXT → COMPASS → MAP → PLAN → TELEGRAPH → REAL-WORLD EXPERIENCE → MEMORY → STAMP → PASSPORT
Canonical architecture rulePassport is the canonical, privacy-aware and context-aware representation of a human throughout Portava. Other surfaces request the appropriate Passport projection instead of rebuilding identity, availability, trust and social context independently.
